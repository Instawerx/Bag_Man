Switching the tab re-scopes the axis, the queue list and the payout shape, because all three are properties of the ruleset.

A row is a queue, not an event

No map name, no venue art, no per-row identity. This is the R18 line and the single most likely thing to drift back toward the reference under pressure — event rows are more visually interesting, which is exactly why they are tempting.

Cold is a variant, not a disabled state

The 1000+ V row sits at 60% with a hollow population pip and reads — quiet. It is still selectable. An empty band must look empty, not broken.

Sorted by population, descending

An informed player self-selects toward the populated option, which shortens waits and self-reinforces. Sorting encodes that without forcing it. Selection is independent of position.

S2 · AFLW_Lobby_DetailPanel 420 × auto @ 150%

Every figure is prefixed ~

The ladder is a generating rule solved per exact field size, and the field is not final until the match starts. A hard number here would be the same class of promise R20 forbids on the stake band.

The min-cash row is marked

1.40× is an input to the solve, not an outcome of it — and it is the number a player uses to decide whether cashing is worth playing for.

Three payout shapes, one rule

Match Play has two positions however many players are in it, so ceil(0.15 × 2) = 1 — always winner-takes-all at 1.90×. BR Squad is nine positions, under ten, so also 1 paid, at 8.55×. BR Solo is 36, so 6 paid, topping at 13.29×. No mode flag decides any of it.

The dead Payouts tab is gone

§16.7 disabled it because TURBO had no placement basis to key on. Match Play has one — a single winner-takes-all row. Defined content, not empty, so the tab is live under both rulesets and the open question closes itself.

The first axis belongs to the ruleset

Match Play offers FORMAT — 1v1 · 3v3 · 5v5 · 8v8, the experiences that exist. Battle Royale offers SQUAD SIZE — Solo · Duo · Squad. Options are read off the live queues, so the axis can never offer something the matchmaker has no queue for. R19 still holds: both axes stay operable at once, only the option set changes.

⚠

Corrections this render forced

found by running the code and the rule instead of copying the docs

0 · The ruleset tabs named two things, and neither one existed

TURBO has zero code and zero assets. Every one of the 18 matches for "turbo" in the tree is WITH_LIBJPEGTURBO — an engine define — or a Tripo model name. There is no timed match structure, no kill-ratio scoring, nothing. match-modes.md §3.3 says so itself, in the words "intended shape". It was never built.

SHOOTOUT has zero code either — and the giveaway is the asset: EXP_AFL_Duel01_Shootout.uasset attaches AFLBattleRoyaleComponent and nothing else. The thing the tab row called SHOOTOUT is Battle Royale, wearing a poker name.

Two match-structure components exist. UAFLRoundManagerComponent — two teams, first to 7 of max 13 — and UAFLBattleRoyaleComponent — N participants, last standing, placement 1..N. (A third, UAFLDeathmatchRankComponent, is named as a sibling in the BR header's own comment and is not on disk. That comment is stale too.)

So the worst part was not the dead tab. The round manager is the most-built mode in the game — six experiences at 3v3, 4v4, 5v5 ×2 leagues and 8v8 ×2 leagues — and the old tab row gave it no way to be queued at all. One tab pointed at a mode with no name of its own; the other pointed at nothing. Renaming to MATCH PLAY and BATTLE ROYALE is what makes the layout addressable, not just accurate.

1 · The S2 example sat on an illegal field size — fixed

§4's ASCII showed a 10-position field. economy-store.md §5.2 names 10, 14, 21, 27 and 34 as the paid-places thresholds and rules that no queue may sit at a field size where the threshold straddles it. The example sat on one.

Re-cut onto BR_36, a field size the project actually builds: 36 players resolves to 36 solo / 18 duo / 9 squad positions. None is a threshold, and all three appear in §5.2's own spot-check table — so every figure on this page is independently checkable. The handoff's §4 now carries the corrected ladder and the reason.

2 · A pre-R40 multiple was still in the SSOT — fixed

§4 showed 1st ~56% ~5.36× at 10 positions. The rule gives 8.10× there, and §5.3's own delta table already said 9 → 10 · 8.55× → 8.10×. The 5.36× traced to a sentence at economy-store.md:240 — a 30%-depth figure that survived the R40 amendment.

Line 240 is corrected. Two neighbouring claims went with it: the block called 9→10 a cliff when at −5.3% it is the mildest of the five (the ones that bite are 14, 21, 27, 34 at 17–23%), and it credited the N=10 threshold to ceil(0.15 × N) incrementing, which it does not — 10 is where the small-field clause hands off, one paid place becoming two.

Working assumptions, flagged not hardened. Rake k = 5% is §16.5's working assumption, not a ruling — every V figure on this page moves if it changes. The four stake presets are §16.1 placeholders. Team payouts are shown as × stake: entry and payout both scale with team size, so × stake reads the same per-player as per-position under an even split.

The naming is now a ruling, not a proposal. R41 landed across eight documents: SHOOTOUT → BATTLE ROYALE is a rename only; TURBO is parked, its design kept in full at match-modes.md §9.7 along with the payout gap that is the real reason it cannot ship — it produces no finishing positions, and a ruleset that cannot be paid cannot be staked. MATCH PLAY took the second slot and is not TURBO renamed. R1, R7, R13 and R36 were amended in place. Docs only — no asset or C++ identifier was renamed, so EXP_AFL_Duel01_Shootout.uasset still carries the old word while attaching the BR component.

Extraction is not a third tab — it is one of Match Play's two round-win routes. The round manager resolves a round on wiping the enemy team OR completing a central-extract bank, so extraction is inside the ruleset's definition, not layered over it. It appears in the Rules tab where it belongs. (An earlier draft of this page called it an objective layer. Reading the component corrected that — and it also surfaced a question the economy SSOT does not close: whether banking a round win earns at all, and how that relates to the extraction run. Flagged there, not answered here.)

Tokens as used

hex is for mockups; linear RGB is authoritative
UI.House.Electric · #1E5AFF LEAD, and now the only blue on these screens. Fills, active states, every edge, every glow, the inactive tab and the unselected preset digits.
Neon edging · Electric at 40 / 70 / 95% Unlit tube · structural rail · lit segment. One hue, three burns — which is how the screen gets depth without a second colour.
UI.House.Violet · #A855F7 ACCENT ONLY. Focus rim, hover edge, CTA halo. Never a fill, never text — shown here as a rim for that reason.
UI.House.Black · #05080F Panel depth and list backing. The ground both radials sit on.
UI.House.Blue · #00ADFF · REMOVED Was tertiary here. Struck from the lobby — it is the free base identity, and identity has no business in chrome.
Glass.Bg.Primary · 12% + neon edge Detail panel and metric cards, blur 28px. The border token moved from white-20% to Electric — glass still, but lit at the seam.
Glass.Tint.Danger. Appears once — the stake field when the value falls outside every band. Semantic, and separate from the accent. Glass.Tint.Danger · rgba(255,80,80,.15)

One blue, three burns. House Blue #00ADFF is out; Electric #1E5AFF took its tertiary job. The inactive tab and the unselected preset digits are now Electric with a neon bloom rather than a second hue — a tube reads dim because it is turned down, not because it is a different colour. Every structural edge on the screen is Electric at 40% (unlit) · 70% (rail) · 95% (lit segment): header foot and commit head are rails, the ruleset baseline is unlit tube with the active tab as its lit segment, metric cards carry a filament along the top edge only, and the detail panel is the one element with a full outline — because it is the focused object and the edge is what says so.

Two places deliberately stay unlit. The min-cash row is ringed in neutral white, not neon — it marks a threshold, and the brand's lit edge would read as "best", which is the opposite of what that row means. The danger tint on an out-of-band stake is semantic and stays red; semantic colour is not the accent.

The blend law survives the change. Electric is core, fill, edge and glow; Violet is rim, focus and hover, and never touches readable core, fill or text. The rim is size-gated on at ≥64px and core-dominant at ≤32px — and nothing in this lobby is 64px, so violet appears nowhere at rest. Hover the QUEUE button or tab into any control to see it. Adding the neon edging did not change that: the edging is Electric, so the accent stayed reserved.

Chrome is not identity — and that is why House Blue had to go. Three distinct blues exist with three distinct owners: Volts #1E5AFF, the free base identity #00ADFF, and the house default identity #5090FF. Spending the free base identity's hue on inactive chrome is exactly the collision the style SSOT calls the classic failure here, and it is invisible on any single screen. Any per-player colour must resolve through the identity registry, never from this table.

One contrast trade, stated rather than hidden. Electric on the black ground measures 3.75:1 — under AA for 14px text. It is used for text in exactly two places, both inactive states, where the lit edge and the fill carry the signal and the glyph is not the thing being read. Every active and every readable value is white at 18:1.

Type. Display is Bahnschrift, a DIN 1451 derivative; data is Cascadia Mono falling back to Consolas; body is Segoe UI Variable. All three ship with Windows, so the target machine renders the intended faces without a webfont — which the artifact CSP would block anyway.
